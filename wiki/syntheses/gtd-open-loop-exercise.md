# The "important exercise" — capture & process one internal agreement

> [!question] Prompt
> Where is the exercise near the beginning of the book that teaches the process to capture and
> process a single internal agreement, and what are its steps?

## Answer

It's the three-step **"Um importante exercício para testar esse modelo"** in **Chapter 1** (~p43),
where Allen has you *feel* what GTD does by running one [[open-loops|laço aberto / internal agreement]]
through the front of the process yourself:

1. **Capture the loop** — *"Anote o projeto ou a situação que mais ocupa sua mente neste momento."*
   Write down whatever is most bothering, distracting, or interesting you right now. → [[capture]]
2. **Define the outcome** — *"Escreva numa única frase, de forma clara, o resultado que pretende
   alcançar."* In one sentence, say what has to happen for this to be marked "concluído." →
   the *desired outcome* half of [[clarify]]
3. **Decide the next action** — *"Anote a próxima ação física necessária para avançar no
   compromisso."* The very next physical, visible thing you'd do if nothing else were on your plate.
   → [[next-action]]

The payoff, in Allen's words:

> Com esse exercício terá experimentado pelo menos um pouco mais de controle, relaxamento, foco e
> motivação. [...] Mas o que levou a isso? a resposta é: pensar. Não muito; apenas o suficiente para
> reforçar seu compromisso com uma leve pressão ou oportunidade. p43

So the exercise is a miniature of the **front of the process only: capture the internal
agreement, then clarify it into `outcome + next action`.** It sits right after the management recipe
for open loops (identify → capture → clarify → decide, p42) and just before the diagnosis of why
things stay on your mind (undefined outcome / undefined next action / no trusted system, p46).

> [!warning] It does NOT exercise the full GTD workflow
> Of the five steps of the [[gtd-method]], the drill covers only the first two, and the second only
> partly:
>
> | GTD step | In the exercise? |
> |---|---|
> | [[capture]] | ✅ step 1 — write down the loop |
> | [[clarify]] | ◐ partial — steps 2–3 define outcome + next action, but skip the decision tree ("exige ação?", two-minute rule, delegate, is it a project?) |
> | [[organize]] | ❌ the next action is never put into a trusted system of reminders |
> | [[reflect]] | ❌ no review |
> | [[engage]] | ❌ you *decide* the action but aren't asked to do it |
>
> The book's own list of "exigências básicas para gerenciar acordos", just before the exercise, has
> three items — capture, clarify, and *"estabelecer um sistema organizado de lembretes que deverão
> ser consultados com regularidade"* (raw line 91). The exercise tests only the first two; the
> third is left out. It is a taste of the clarity GTD produces, not a run of the method.

## Supporting detail

**Location.** Raw source: `raw/allen-gtd-book-notes.md`, under the heading *"Um importante exercício
para testar esse modelo"* (lines 97–103), Chapter 1, ~p43.

**Why "think about it, not think it."** The closing note explains the mechanism — the value came from
thinking *about* the problem (results and actions) rather than churning *on* it:

> As pessoas pensam demais, mas na maior parte do tempo, pensam em um problema, não a respeito dele.
> Você deve concentrar seu pensamento em ações e resultados [...]. A reação é automática; a reflexão,
> não. p43

> [!note] Don't confuse it with the later exercise
> A **second, similar** hands-on exercise appears in **Chapter 3** (~p93, raw line 406) — see
> [[natural-planning-exercise]]. That one runs the five phases of the [[natural-planning-model]]
> (purpose → vision → brainstorm → organize → next action) on a whole project. Those are the five
> *planning* phases, **not** the five GTD workflow steps — so neither exercise runs the full GTD
> workflow. The one here is the earlier, simpler drill on a **single** open loop.
