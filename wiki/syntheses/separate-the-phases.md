# Separate the phases — don't run GTD all at once

> [!question] Prompt
> What does the GTD book say about not doing the whole process in one go?

## Answer

The five steps of [[gtd-method|GTD]] — [[capture|capturar]], [[clarify|esclarecer]], [[organize|organizar]],
[[reflect|refletir]], [[engage|engajar]] — are **distinct phases meant to be done separately, not
simultaneously.** Allen names blending them as *the* reason people fail to get organized:

> O motivo pelo qual muitos não conseguem se organizar é porque tentam executar todas as fases ao mesmo tempo. Quando param para preparar uma lista já tentam capturar, esclarecer e organizar junto. p59

*(The reason many can't get organized is that they try to carry out all the phases at the same time.
When they stop to prepare a list, they're already trying to capture, clarify, and organize all together.)*

The discipline: **capture everything first, then clarify, then organize** — as three separate passes.

## Supporting detail

Each phase demands a different mode of thinking, so mixing them creates friction:

- **[[capture|Capturar]]** — fast, non-judgmental collection; empty your head into an inbox, decide nothing.
- **[[clarify|Esclarecer]]** — decision work; "Isto exige ação?" and, if so, the [[next-action|próxima ação]].
- **[[organize|Organizar]]** — filing the results into the right buckets (calendar, context lists,
  [[open-loops|Waiting For]], Someday/Maybe, reference).

Stopping mid-capture to decide *what to do* about an item (clarify) or *where it goes* (organize) breaks
the flow of all three. This is also why the [[weekly-review|Revisão Semanal]] is a dedicated sitting that
re-runs capture → clarify → organize → reflect as its own block, rather than something squeezed in
continuously.
